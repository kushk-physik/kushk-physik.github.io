2021 – 2026
PhD Research, Universidade Federal Fluminense (UFF), Brazil
Supervisor: Prof. Gabriel S. Denicol
  • Derived relativistic dissipative magnetohydrodynamics for a two-component gas from the Boltzmann equation using the method of moments, and showed that the longitudinal and transverse shear-stress components relax differently, with oscillatory relaxation driven by the gyromotion of charged particles (Phys. Rev. D 109, 096021).
  • Carried out a linear causality and stability analysis of this theory for perturbations parallel and transverse to the magnetic field (Phys. Rev. D 112, 076035), and identified the onset of a relativistic firehose instability around anisotropic backgrounds (EPJ Web Conf. 364, 14002).
  • Derived coupled equations for the charge current and shear stress of a resistive two-component plasma, giving a generalized Ohm’s law with nonlinear electromagnetic coupling (Phys. Rev. D 113, 036021), and showed that an electric field alone generates momentum anisotropy (J. Subatomic Part. Cosmol. 6, 100530).
  • Built an effective kinetic model, solved numerically in Python, for the magnetic-field-induced anisotropic gluon pressure in the pre-equilibrium stage of heavy-ion collisions, finding that the response depends on both the strength and the lifetime of the field (arXiv:2609.05923).
Oct – Nov 2024
Visiting Researcher, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
Host: Prof. Jorge Noronha
  • Derived nonlinear causality constraints on the transport coefficients, dissipative stresses and electromagnetic fields of massless dissipative magnetohydrodynamics.
2016 – 2021
Master’s Research, NISER, Bhubaneswar, India
Supervisor: Prof. Amaresh Jaiswal
  • Thesis (2020 – 2021): “Modelling transport coefficients in presence of electromagnetic field using Chapman–Enskog formalism”.
  • Derived second-order viscous, non-resistive relativistic magnetohydrodynamic equations for the dissipative quantities in the relaxation-time approximation, and obtained field-dependent viscosities and electrical conductivity with the Chapman–Enskog method.
